The learning rate controls how big a step the optimizer takes in parameter space. Choosing the right schedule can dramatically improve training.

Why Schedule the Learning Rate?

Early in training, you want to explore the parameter space quickly with large steps. Later, you need smaller steps to fine-tune and converge to a good minimum.

Key Principle: Start fast, end slow. Large learning rates early for exploration, small learning rates later for refinement.

Scheduler Types

None (Constant)

Learning rate stays the same throughout training. Simple but often suboptimal. May overshoot the minimum or converge slowly.

Step Decay

Reduces learning rate by a factor (e.g., 0.1) at specific epochs. Common schedule: reduce at 50% and 75% of training.

  • Simple and effective
  • Sudden drops can cause training instability
  • Widely used in computer vision

Cosine Annealing

Smoothly decreases learning rate following a cosine curve. Provides gradual reduction without sudden jumps.

  • Smooth transitions
  • Popular in modern architectures
  • Can be combined with warm restarts

Exponential Decay

Multiplies learning rate by a constant factor each epoch. Provides continuous, smooth decay.

  • Gradual reduction
  • May decay too quickly or slowly depending on factor
